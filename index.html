Strategy & Market Research

Kellogg's in India:
From Failure to Market Leader

A deep strategic analysis of how Kellogg's turned a failed market entry into a 50%+ market share leadership position in India.

Market Research Strategic Analysis
0.01%

50%+
Market Share in Indian Breakfast Cereals
The Opportunity

Kellogg's entered India with the same products and messaging it used in the US, criticized traditional Indian breakfast habits, and nearly failed. The question was why — and what eventually turned it around.

The Result

Kellogg's penetration grew from 0.01% to nearly 14% of the Indian population after revising its marketing strategies, and now holds 50%+ of the Indian breakfast cereal market.

What I Did

Synthesized 48 sources across a 34 page structured analysis applying 6 frameworks and competitive benchmarking

01
Research & Sourcing
Synthesized 48 credible sources including reports, articles, and case studies
02
Framework Analysis
Applied 6 frameworks: SWOT, PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, STP, Force Field, 4Ps
03
Competitive Benchmarking
Benchmarked against 3+ Indian breakfast cereal competitors across key dimensions
04
Gap Identification
Analyzed localization gaps in product mix, pricing, distribution, and promotional strategy
05
Insights & Conclusions
Synthesized findings and derived strategic insights for market turnaround
Key Findings
Initial approach criticized Indian breakfast habits and ignored cultural context
Products were seen as "too foreign" and not suited to Indian tastes
Pricing was misaligned with local purchasing power
Distribution was limited to urban, modern trade channels
Messaging failed to connect with Indian families and values
Key Finding: Kellogg's initial failure came from copying its US strategy without adaptation. Its turnaround came entirely from cultural localization across product, pricing, and distribution.

Brand & Marketing Strategy

Cycarbon —
Bicycle Brand Marketing Simulation

Founded and ran Cycarbon, a bicycle company, through a 7-quarter marketing simulation, making segmentation, branding, pricing, advertising, and sales channel decisions across four global markets against live competitors.

Market Segmentation Pricing Strategy Competitive Benchmarking
7
Quarters simulated
4
Global markets entered
5
Brands launched
The Opportunity

Enter a competitive global bicycle market as a brand-new company, Cycarbon, choosing target segments and building brands from scratch to compete against established players across Amsterdam, Bangalore, New York City, and Rio de Janeiro.

The Result

Grew Cycarbon from the lowest-performing company in the market in Quarter 2 to the highest total performance by Quarter 6, before a pricing misstep in the Speed segment dropped performance again in Quarter 7 — a real lesson in price sensitivity.

What I Did

Made end-to-end brand and go-to-market decisions each quarter, guided by competitor data and Balanced Scorecard results

01
Market Entry
Selected Recreation as the top segment for highest demand, opened first store in New York City
02
Brand Development
Launched EaseRides, then rebuilt it into ConvenientRides using competitor benchmarking
03
Pricing & Advertising
Iterated rebate structures and ad hierarchies each quarter using competitor ad and brand scores
04
Market Expansion
Entered the Speed segment with FastRides and RaceFast, expanded to Amsterdam, Bangalore, and Rio de Janeiro
05
Performance Analysis
Used each quarter's Balanced Scorecard to diagnose weaknesses and course-correct strategy
Key Findings
The first brand and ad underperformed against competitors like Luxe, dragging financial and market performance to the lowest in the market
Benchmarking new brands directly against top competitor products improved brand judgment scores each quarter
Reducing ad clutter and mirroring high-performing competitor ad structures steadily improved ad judgment
Balanced Scorecard performance climbed from worst-in-market in Quarter 2 to best-in-market by Quarter 6
A price increase on Fast Rides in Quarter 6 lowered demand and dropped performance in Quarter 7, confirming strong price sensitivity in the Speed segment
Key Insight: Consistent competitor benchmarking, not intuition, was what turned the lowest-performing brand in the market into the strongest.
